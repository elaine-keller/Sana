More glucose graph examples — food combinations

The remaining course examples

Add fat + protein to oats

Oats with fruit purée → spike to 140+
Same oats + 1 tbsp butter + egg → stays flat ~80

Adding fat and protein to the exact same carb base dramatically flattens the curve. Oatmeal alone is pure glucose. With an egg and butter, it behaves like a balanced meal.

Always pair fruit with fat

Green apple alone → spike to ~110
Apple + handful of almonds → barely moves

Fruit is healthy — but never eat it alone. Fat from almonds, nut butter, or cheese acts as a brake on glucose absorption. This applies to all fruit, especially in the morning.

Smoothies — what goes in matters

Apple + banana + strawberry → spike to 155+
Avocado + protein + blueberries → barely moves

A smoothie full of fruit is liquid sugar. Swap most fruit for vegetables, add fat (avocado) and protein, and keep to one small fruit. The difference is enormous.

Rice cakes aren't "free"

Plain rice cake → spike +60 above baseline
Rice cake + 2 tbsp nut butter → significantly smaller spike

Rice cakes, amaranth crackers, and similar "healthy" snacks are almost pure starch. They look harmless on a nutrition label but spike glucose hard. Always add fat or protein.

Eat fiber first (the salad trick)

Pizza alone → stays elevated for hours
Big salad first, then pizza → much flatter curve

Eating a large green salad before a high-carb meal coats the gut with fiber, physically slowing glucose absorption. This works for pasta, pizza, rice — any high-glycemic food.

Combine white rice with fiber

White rice alone → spike to ~120+
Rice + beans → stays under 80

White rice is one of the fastest glucose-spiking foods. Beans are a legume with high fiber — combining them creates a completely different metabolic response. This is why traditional rice-and-beans combinations exist in most food cultures.

The 4 glucose "brakes" — summary

1Protein — slows gastric emptying and blunts insulin response. Always start a meal with protein.
2Fat — slows glucose absorption. Add to every carb you eat (nut butter, olive oil, avocado, butter, cheese).
3Fiber — physically coats the gut. Eat vegetables first, before carbs. The more fiber in a meal, the flatter the curve.
4Vinegar — 1 tbsp apple cider vinegar in water before a high-carb meal measurably reduces the spike.

The goal is never to eliminate carbs — it's to never eat them alone. Every carb needs at least one brake. Timing matters too: carbs eaten after protein and vegetables behave very differently than the same carbs eaten first.
