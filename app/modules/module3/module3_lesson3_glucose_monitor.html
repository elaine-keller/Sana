How to use a glucose monitor

Lab work gives you a single snapshot. A glucose monitor shows you the whole movie — what happens across the entire day, in real time, in response to your exact food, sleep, stress, and exercise. Everyone responds differently to the same food, which is why this data is irreplaceable.

Optimal glucose ranges — from the course slide

When measuredOptimal level
Fasting72–85 mg/dL
Pre-prandial (just before eating)72–90 mg/dL
Post-prandial peak (45–60 min after eating)≤ 110 mg/dL (max 120)
Daily average90–100 mg/dL

Program your sensor or app to show the green zone as 75–110. Any reading outside that range is a spike worth investigating. Standard guidelines say under 140 is fine — that is not the goal here.

Two tools — glucometer vs. continuous sensor

Glucometer (finger-prick)

  • Available at any pharmacy or supermarket
  • ~$25–30 USD one-time cost
  • Gives a single point-in-time reading
  • Cannot measure during sleep or exercise
  • Requires manual logging to see trends
  • Measure fasting, pre-meal, 45 min, 60 min, 2 hrs, 3 hrs after eating — for one week to build a trend

Freestyle Libre sensor (CGM)

  • Worn on back of upper arm for 14 days
  • Measures glucose 24 hrs including during sleep
  • ~$60 USD per sensor (14-day use)
  • Scan with the FreeStyle LibreLink app or the reader device
  • Slight margin of error (interstitial fluid, not blood)
  • Tip: put it on and wait 2 days before activating — lets initial inflammation subside for more accurate readings

Download the tracking sheet from the course to log these readings. A week of data reveals your personal patterns — which foods spike you, how fast you recover, and whether you're achieving metabolic flexibility.

What to look for in your glucose curve

✓Stay in the green zone. Curve should remain between 75–110 as much as possible. Consistently above range = chronically elevated glucose.
✓Keep the line flat. Many spikes throughout the day = constant grazing or foods your body can't handle.
✓Fast recovery. A spike is less dangerous if glucose returns to baseline quickly. Slow recovery = reduced metabolic flexibility.
✓Identify your triggers. Note what caused each spike — food, exercise (even intense cardio can spike to 185+), stress, poor sleep.
!Nocturnal hypoglycemia. Glucose dropping and staying low overnight = real issue. Can be caused by alcohol, intense evening exercise, sleep position on the sensor, or the Dawn Effect.

Dawn Effect: some people following low-carb or keto diets show high fasting glucose (90–100+) despite eating well. This is a physiological response — check fasting insulin to distinguish it from true IR. If insulin is normal, don't panic about the glucose reading alone.

Real-world examples from glucose graphs

Liquid vs. whole

Orange juice → big spike
Whole orange → flat curve

Fiber in whole fruit slows glucose absorption. Juice removes the fiber — same sugar, 3× the spike. Same applies to smoothies heavy on fruit vs. vegetables.

Liquid carbs to avoid

Gin & tonic → spikevsGin & soda → flat

Tonic water is loaded with sugar. Club soda is not. The alcohol is the same — the mixer makes all the difference. Same principle for sweetened coffees, sports drinks, and quinine water.

Timing / meal order

Pineapple as a snack → spike
Pineapple as dessert → smaller spike

Eating carbs after protein + fat + fiber significantly blunts the glucose response. Same food, same portion — completely different impact depending on when in the meal it's eaten.

The vinegar hack

Frozen yogurt alone → 150+ spike
Apple cider vinegar first → stays ~120

1 tbsp apple cider vinegar in water before a high-carb meal measurably reduces the glucose spike. More on this in the method modules.

Sleep quality

Cappuccino after bad sleep → spike
Same cappuccino after good sleep → flat

The exact same food causes a meaningfully different glucose response depending on how well you slept. Sleep is metabolic — not optional.

Combine carbs with fat/protein

Oatmeal alone → spike
Oatmeal after eggs → flat

A plain bowl of oatmeal is pure glucose. The same portion eaten after scrambled eggs causes a fraction of the spike. Apple alone vs. apple + almonds shows the same principle.
