The Reset 5 method — action plan overview

The functional approach — not treating symptoms, but reversing the root cause. The goal is to make cells more efficient so they need less insulin to balance blood sugar, and to teach the body to use fat as fuel.

Why conventional treatment doesn't reverse IR

✗Metformin — reduces symptoms but depletes vitamin B12 and other vitamins essential for metabolism, hormones, and nerve function. Long-term use has significant side effects.
✗Birth control pills — often prescribed for PCOS-related IR but suppress rather than regulate hormones.
✗"Eat less, move more" — treats the body like a calculator. Calories matter, but hormones matter more. Insulin decides what happens with every calorie.
✗High-carb diet for hypoglycemia — commonly advised but reinforces the glucose-insulin rollercoaster instead of breaking it.
✗Statins, BP meds, immunosuppressants — each addresses a downstream symptom without touching the metabolic root cause.

The real goal — metabolic flexibility

The body has two fuel sources. A metabolically flexible person can switch between them freely. Only 12% of the population currently has this ability — but it can be trained.

Fuel 1 — Carbohydrates / glucose

Used immediately after eating. Fast-burning, quick to deplete. Most people are stuck using only this source.

Fuel 2 — Body fat

Used during fasting, between meals, and at rest. Nearly unlimited supply. Produces stable, long-lasting energy.

Signs you have metabolic flexibility

Can go several hours between meals without feeling unwell or irritable
Insulin returns to baseline between meals — not chronically elevated
Energy levels stay stable throughout the day
Can burn stored body fat between meals and overnight
Better exercise performance and muscle building capacity
Can eat efficiently without gaining weight or retaining fluid

The Reset 5 — nutrition pillar spelled out

The word RESET maps directly to the nutrition principles. Each letter is a rule to follow:

R
Reduce & rotate your carbohydrates
Don't eliminate — manage. Learn to choose, combine, time, and portion carbs so they don't spike glucose.
E
Choose protein
Prioritize protein at every meal. It's the anchor of metabolic stability — satiating, muscle-preserving, and insulin-friendly.
S
Always vegetables
Add vegetables in fiber form at every meal. Fiber is the primary brake on glucose absorption.
E
Balance fat
Not too much, not too little — calibrated. Healthy fat slows digestion and prevents glucose spikes, but portions matter.
T
Time — between meals and fasting windows
Give the body time between meals for insulin to drop and fat burning to begin. This is the foundation of intermittent fasting.
