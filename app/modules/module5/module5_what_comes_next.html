Module 5 is the bridge between completing the program and living it permanently. Two key things: knowing whether you've actually reversed IR, and internalizing the rules that carry you forward for life.

6 signs you've reversed insulin resistance

If most of these are true, your blood sugar is balanced and IR is reversing or reversed:

1
Stable energy all day
No afternoon crashes. No mood swings tied to meals. No desperate need for a nap at 3pm.
2
Sugar cravings have dropped
You can want a cookie without needing the whole box. Desire vs. compulsion — you can tell the difference now.
3
Energy doesn't depend on food
You can go 4–5 hours between meals without shakiness, weakness, mood changes, or desperation.
4
Sleeping through the night
You fall asleep when you want, sleep through, and wake up feeling genuinely rested.
5
Hunger & fullness signals are back
You feel satisfied after a meal. You can distinguish between real hunger and anxiety. You stop eating because you're full.
6
Body responds to effort
Exercise builds muscle visibly. Weight loss is achievable when you try. You don't gain 3 kg from one restaurant meal.

Even after reversal, continue eating as if you have IR. It's not a diet — it's the lifestyle. Blood sugar moves constantly and the habits that reversed IR are the same ones that keep it reversed.

The rules to keep for life — master summary

This is the condensed version of everything in the program. If you could only keep one page, this is it.

1
Use a glucometer — find what works for you
No perfect diet exists universally. Your glucose data is the truth. Test your own responses to foods and build your personal version of this plan.
2
Avoid refined carbs and refined fats — permanently
Industrial seed oils (canola, corn, soy) and anything made from flour that comes in a package. These are the two biggest dietary drivers of IR.
3
No liquid carbs — for you or your kids
Juice, soda, sports drinks, sweetened coffees. Children are not protected from this damage — they're more vulnerable because it accelerates disease earlier.
4
Always dress your carbs and fruit
Never eat alone. Always paired with protein, fat, or fiber. This rule will echo in your head for life — and that's the point.
5
Lower carb frequency — 1 to 2 times per day max
Concentrate all your carb portions into one or two meals. This reduces daily insulin spikes and deepens metabolic flexibility over time.
6
Include apple cider vinegar strategically
Before your highest-carb meal when possible. Find a form you actually enjoy so it's sustainable. Capsules if you truly can't tolerate the taste — not gummies.
7
Prioritize quality protein
Satiety, muscle building, glucose stability — protein does all three. Focus on quality, not just quantity.
8
Moderate fat — use your body's stores
Even good fat should be moderate. The goal is to use stored body fat for energy between meals, not just the fat you eat.
9
3 fixed meals — no snacking
Every bite triggers insulin. Even healthy snacks prevent the fat-burning windows you've worked to create. Exception: people with hypoglycemia still managing the transition.
10
Always fast at least 12 hours
Between dinner and breakfast, minimum. Extend to 14–16 hours when possible. This is non-negotiable as a baseline habit.
11
Exercise — especially walk after meals
A short walk after eating can significantly lower post-meal glucose. Exercise is also the primary activator of metabolic flexibility.
12
Protect your sleep
Sleep is metabolic medicine. One bad night raises IR risk by 30%. Everything you've built metabolically gets undermined by chronic poor sleep.
13
Manage stress and emotions
Stress raises blood glucose as powerfully as food does. Cortisol is a metabolic hormone. What you think and feel matters as much as what you eat. Find what genuinely makes you happy and protect it.
